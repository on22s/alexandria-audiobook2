## 1. The H100 cannot train FP8 — and could not reliably infer it either

The LR ladder queued for the H100 (`~/qwen38_lr_ladder_h100_20260901.sh`) died
on launch:

> ValueError: The model you are trying to fine-tune is quantized with fp8 but
> that quantization method do not support training.

That box holds **only** `Qwen3.8-27B-FP8`; the BF16 weights exist solely on the
A100. Two attempts to at least *evaluate* FP8 there also failed:

1. `HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1` (which I set) blocks the `kernels-community/finegrained-fp8`
   kernel fetch that FP8 inference needs on Hopper. Every forward pass errored,
   four retries deep — **and the run still wrote an artifact**: 766 rows,
   `predicted=None` and `raw_response=None` on every one, summarised as
   **`+0.0 points, +0/-0 of 383, p=1`**. That is two empty arms agreeing, not a
   null result. Deleted, not committed.
2. With kernels online and `CUDA_HOME=/usr/local/cuda-13` (nvcc IS installed —
   transformers only probes `/usr/local/cuda/bin`, which is runtime-only), it
   loaded and then **hung**: 0% CPU, 0% GPU, 18 minutes, no output.

**Blast radius, checked rather than assumed.** The fine-grained FP8 kernel is a
Hopper path. The A100 never fetches it, and its existing FP8 artifacts carry
716–735 non-null rows of 766. Of 23 `distill_eval__*.json` on the A100, the only
zero-row files are 4 named `.INVALID.json` / `.CORRECTION.json` — deliberate
retraction markers. **So this hazard is confined to the H100.**

Still worth a look: `raw_response` is the literal string `"None"` on 11 of 14
qwen38 artifacts. Failure mode 1 produces exactly that shape. A `+0.0 / p=1`
paired line with `+0/-0` flips is the tell.

## 2. H100 repurposed: Qwen3-14B BF16 training-strength ladder

Template, batch shape, precision and adapter placement have all come back null
for the 3.8 regression. Training *strength* was the surviving untested
hypothesis, and it is testable on any base. `Qwen3-14B` was already cached
(28 G) and is the base behind goal 1.3.

**Measured** (index18, 88 scoreable lines, paired against the same base):

| arm | base | tuned | Δ | flips | p | unanswered |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| lr 2e-5, r16 (shipped) | 62.5% | 71.6% | +9.1 | +14/−6 | 0.1153 | 8 |
| lr 5e-5, r16 | 61.4% | 73.9% | **+12.5** | +16/−5 | **0.0266** | 5 |
| lr 2e-4, r16 | 61.4% | 68.2% | +6.8 | +16/−10 | 0.3269 | — |
| lr 5e-6, r16 | *eval in flight at 08:30 UTC* | | | | | |

**The shape is an inverted U, not a ramp.** Overnight I reported the first two
arms and inferred "higher lr is better, consistent with undertraining". The
2e-4 arm falsified that: the gain peaks at 5e-5 and falls off by 2e-4, where the
regressions more than double (−5 → −10). Treat the earlier framing as retracted.

What the numbers support: the shipped 2e-5 is on the low side of a peak near
5e-5 on this base. What they do **not** support: any claim about Qwen3.8, which
is a different architecture and was not in this ladder. Also note the base arm
scored 55/88 then 54/88 twice — roughly one row of decoding nondeterminism sits
under every delta here.

Conditional accuracy (rows where the model answered) is the sharper contrast:
base 67.7–69.4%, tuned 82.3–85.5%.
