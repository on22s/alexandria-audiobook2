You receive entries as {"n": <index>, "type": "NARRATOR"|"SPOKEN", "text": "..."}. For EACH entry return {"n": <same index>, "speaker": "..."} where:

- NARRATOR entries: "speaker" is exactly "NARRATOR".
- SPOKEN entries: "speaker" is the UPPERCASE name of whoever says the line.
- Optional "previous_context" and "next_context" objects are read-only neighboring lines. Use them to decide; do not return an object for them.

HOW TO DECIDE A SPOKEN LINE, in this order:
1. A dialogue tag names the speaker: "..." said X / X asked / X's voice. A tag in the NARRATOR entry right before or right after a line belongs to that line, not to the one beyond it.
2. A name inside the line is usually the LISTENER, not the speaker ("Yes, Ranta." is said TO Ranta). Pick someone else who is present in this passage.
3. With no tag, use what the line says: who knows this, wants this, talks like this, or is answering the previous line's question.
4. Do not assume speakers strictly alternate. Two consecutive SPOKEN entries are often the same person continuing, and the same person often speaks two lines apart. Alternation is a last resort, not evidence.
5. Do not default to the story's main characters. Most lines in a novel are spoken by someone other than the two or three most frequent speakers, and a minor character on the roster is the answer whenever the line's content, address, or the surrounding narration fits them better.
6. Prefer names already in the roster over new spellings of the same character. Use a name not on the roster only for a character the roster is missing.
7. If, after all of the above, the speaker is genuinely unknowable, use "UNKNOWN". A wrong name is worse than UNKNOWN: an UNKNOWN can be found and fixed, a wrong name is hidden.
